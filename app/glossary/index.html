The words the record uses

A meeting says “warrant article” and “free cash” dozens of times and never stops to say what they mean. These are words the record uses and seldom explains — 47 entries, 47 of them said on this edition’s tapes — each in plain language, with where it applies, the public source to read, and how often the record says it, town by town. Every count opens the search that finds it.

The definitions were written with Claude (Anthropic, claude-opus-5-5) — on Anthropic’s servers, through the coding assistant the developers used while writing this code; never at press time, never in your browser. Each names the public source it draws on, and where the two differ the source is the authority. No person has yet read them against their sources — until one has, read each entry as a first draft and its source as the definition. A correction is dated beside the entry it changes. The counts are the record’s own, whole-word over the transcripts of the towns each word belongs to: no model counts them. Our AI Constitution keeps the ledger.

Who decides

Select Board

BrooklineMassachusetts

The town’s elected executive: in Brookline, five members elected to three-year terms. It calls Town Meeting and writes its warrant, hires the Town Administrator, sets the budget’s guidelines, appoints most boards, grants licenses and serves as the police commissioners. Many towns once called it the Board of Selectmen.

read the source Brookline — Select Board

on the record Brookline: 740 times in 14 meetings, first on December 9, 2025

Town Meeting

BrooklineMassachusetts

The town’s legislature. Brookline’s is a representative Town Meeting: 255 members elected by precinct — fifteen from each of seventeen, for three-year terms — joined by members who sit by office (the Select Board, the Moderator, the Town Clerk, and any state legislator who lives in town). It passes the budget and every bylaw. The annual meeting comes in late spring and a special one usually in the fall; anyone may watch, only members vote.

Advisory Committee

Brookline

Town Meeting’s finance committee: twenty to thirty residents, appointed by the Moderator, who hold hearings on every warrant article — the budget included — and recommend how Town Meeting should vote. It alone may approve a transfer from the reserve fund.

How a meeting runs

warrant, warrant article

BrooklineMassachusetts

The warrant is the official list of what a Town Meeting may act on, issued by the Select Board; each item on it is a warrant article — the budget, a bylaw, a zoning change, a resolution. An article reaches the warrant from the Select Board or by a petition of the town’s voters, and Town Meeting may act only on what its warrant says.

read the source Brookline — Town Meeting · M.G.L. c. 39 § 10

favorable action, no action

Brookline

How a board recommends a warrant article to Town Meeting: favorable action — adopt it, usually in the words of a motion it offers — or no action. The recommendation is advice; Town Meeting decides by its own vote.

reconsideration

Massachusetts

Massachusetts

The number of members who must be present before a body may act — under the Open Meeting Law, a simple majority, unless a law, a charter or a bylaw sets a different number.

roll call

Massachusetts

A vote taken by calling each member by name, so that how each one voted — yes, no, abstain — is on the record. The record reads the roll calls it can hear on the tape; the official minutes are the last word.

Massachusetts

A session a board must advertise in advance and open to anyone before it decides certain things — a zoning change, a permit, a license — so that the people affected can be heard.

public comment

Massachusetts

Time a board sets aside for anyone to speak, on the board’s own rules — usually a few minutes each. The Open Meeting Law requires a meeting to be open to the public; whether and when the public speaks is the chair’s to decide.

read the source Brookline — Select Board · the Attorney General — the Open Meeting Law

executive session

Massachusetts

The part of a meeting a public body may hold in private — only for a reason the Open Meeting Law lists, among them a person’s reputation, negotiations with staff outside a union, and — when an open meeting may hurt the body’s position and the chair says so — strategy for collective bargaining or litigation and the purchase or lease of real estate — entered by a roll-call vote in open session, with the reason stated.

read the source M.G.L. c. 30A § 21

Open Meeting Law

Massachusetts

The state law that requires a public body to meet in the open, to post each meeting forty-eight hours ahead (weekends and holidays aside), and to keep minutes — with a short list of reasons it may meet in executive session.

appropriation

Massachusetts

A vote of Town Meeting or a city council that authorizes spending a set amount for a set purpose. Most public money is spent only as an appropriation allows; grants, gifts and revolving funds have rules of their own.

Proposition 2½

Massachusetts

The 1980 state law that caps a community’s property taxes. The most it may levy in a year — its levy limit — may grow by 2.5 percent plus new growth, and the limit may never pass 2.5 percent of the full value of all its taxable property, the levy ceiling. Voters can raise the limit with an override, or tax beyond it — even beyond the ceiling — for one project’s debt with a debt exclusion.

read the source M.G.L. c. 59 § 21C · the state’s Municipal Finance Glossary

on the record Brookline: 20 times in 4 meetings, first on December 9, 2025 · Boston: 7 times in 1 meeting, first on July 8, 2026

levy, levy limit

Massachusetts

The levy is what a community raises in property taxes in a year. The levy limit is the most Proposition 2½ lets it raise: last year’s limit, plus 2.5 percent, plus new growth, plus any override its voters approved. A debt exclusion’s payments ride on top of the limit, only while the debt lasts — so the law reads; the state’s glossary counts exclusions into the limit.

read the source M.G.L. c. 59 § 21C · the state’s Municipal Finance Glossary

on the record Brookline: 71 times in 5 meetings, first on December 9, 2025 · Boston: 4 times in 1 meeting, first on July 8, 2026

new growth

Massachusetts

Tax from property new to the tax rolls — new buildings, additions, renovations — which a community may add to its levy limit on top of the 2.5 percent. A rise in market values alone is not new growth.

read the source the state’s Municipal Finance Glossary

on the record Brookline: 26 times in 3 meetings, first on December 9, 2025 · Boston: 1 time in 1 meeting, first on July 8, 2026

override

MassachusettsBrookline

A ballot question that asks voters to raise the levy limit permanently, by a stated amount for a stated purpose. It needs a majority at the polls, and it can never lift the levy past the levy ceiling. The money it raises is then appropriated like any other.

read the source the state’s Municipal Finance Glossary · Brookline — the FY 2027–2029 override guide

on the record Brookline: 326 times in 11 meetings, first on December 9, 2025 · Boston: 1 time in 1 meeting, first on September 22, 2026

debt exclusion

Massachusetts

A ballot question that lets a community tax above its levy limit only to pay the debt on one project — a school, a fire station — and only until that debt is paid. Unlike an override, it ends.

read the source the state’s Municipal Finance Glossary

free cash

Massachusetts

Unrestricted money left over from the last fiscal year — revenue above its estimate, budget lines not spent — once the state certifies the amount. It is often spent on one-time costs or put into reserves, by a vote of Town Meeting or the council.

read the source the state’s Municipal Finance Glossary

stabilization fund

Massachusetts

A community’s savings for the future — an emergency, a lean year, a large capital cost. Creating a fund or changing its purpose takes a two-thirds vote of Town Meeting or the council. Spending from the general stabilization fund takes two thirds; a fund set up for one named purpose can be spent by a simple majority. Putting money in takes a simple majority.

read the source the state’s Municipal Finance Glossary · M.G.L. c. 40 § 5B

reserve fund

MassachusettsBrookline

A small sum set aside in the budget — no more than 5 percent of the last year’s levy — for costs no one could foresee. In a town the finance committee approves each transfer from it; in Brookline, the Advisory Committee.

Community Preservation Act (CPA)

Massachusetts

A state law a community may adopt by ballot: a surcharge of up to 3 percent on property taxes, matched in part by the state, spent only on open space, historic preservation, affordable housing and outdoor recreation, on the advice of a local Community Preservation Committee.

What gets built, and where

bylaw

Massachusetts

A town’s own local law — its general bylaws and its zoning bylaw — adopted or changed by Town Meeting. A city’s local laws are ordinances, passed by its council.

read the source Brookline — Town Meeting

on the record Brookline: 204 times in 10 meetings, first on December 9, 2025

zoning, zoning bylaw

Massachusetts

The rules for what may be built where — uses, heights, setbacks, parking. A town sets them in its zoning bylaw under the state’s Zoning Act; most changes take a two-thirds vote of Town Meeting (in another city, of its council), some housing changes a simple majority. Boston is the exception: its zoning code rests on a state law of its own, and its Zoning Commission, with the mayor — not the City Council — adopts changes.

read the source M.G.L. c. 40A (the Zoning Act) · Boston — the Enabling Act (St. 1956, c. 665)

variance

Massachusetts

Permission to depart from the zoning’s own terms — a setback, a height — granted only where the soil, shape or topography of that particular lot, and not the district around it, would make the rule a substantial hardship, and only if the relief does no substantial harm to the public good or to the bylaw’s purpose. A use the district forbids needs a bylaw that allows use variances at all.

read the source M.G.L. c. 40A § 10

Chapter 40B

Massachusetts

The state law that lets a developer who sets aside a share of new homes as affordable ask the Zoning Board of Appeals for one comprehensive permit in place of the separate local approvals. If the board refuses, or sets conditions that make the homes uneconomic, the developer can appeal to the state’s Housing Appeals Committee, which can overrule it — unless the community has met the law’s safe harbors, chiefly 10 percent of its housing counted as affordable.

read the source M.G.L. c. 40B §§ 20–23 · § 22 (the appeal)

on the record Brookline: 11 times in 3 meetings, first on March 10, 2026

MBTA Communities (Section 3A)

Massachusetts

The 2021 state law that requires 177 cities and towns in and around the MBTA’s service area — Boston, served by the MBTA but outside the Zoning Act, is not one of them — to have at least one zoning district of reasonable size (at least 15 homes an acre, and where a town has a station, in part within half a mile of it) where multifamily housing open to families is allowed as of right, without a special permit.
